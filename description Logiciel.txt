# CHARCOT SENTINEL User Tutorial (English)

## 1. What the software does
CHARCOT SENTINEL is an onboard emergency coordination system for incident management on LE COMMANDANT CHARCOT.  
It centralizes:
- Incident activation and tracking
- Team coordination
- Muster and accountability
- Abandon ship preparation
- Shared logs, reports, and internal messaging
- Deck plans and location support

It is designed for fast decision-making under pressure, with one operational picture shared across Bridge, OSC, ECR, and support stations.

---

## 2. Core operating logic
The system follows a clear operational flow:

1. Detect and classify the emergency (Fire, Flooding, Pollution, Medical, Muster, Abandon).
2. Activate the incident from Home (hold-to-activate to reduce accidental triggers).
3. Navigate to the dedicated incident page.
4. Enter situation data (location, severity, hazards, casualties, barriers/doors, etc.).
5. Track team status and checklist progress.
6. Update accountability (passengers/crew present, missing, embarked, etc.).
7. Keep communication and logs synchronized across stations.
8. Generate/save reports and maintain timeline traceability.

Every key action is intended to be time-stamped and visible for command continuity.

## 4. How to use each key module

## Fire (Code Bravo)
Use this page for tactical fire control:
- Fire location and OSC meeting point
- Staircase and locker availability
- Evacuation zone selector by deck and MVZ
- Fire doors and watertight doors closure tracking
- Ventilation stop/high pressure status
- DCP/NOVEC activation tracking
- Casualties + link to Medical
- Fire teams and protection teams management
- Checklist and report save

Best practice: Fill location and boundaries first, then containment barriers, then team deployment.

## Flooding (Code Delta)
Use for ingress and stability management:
- Source and location of flooding
- Severity and water level
- Hull integrity state
- List and trim inputs
- Bilge pumps status
- Team checklist
- Compartment map with severity levels by deck

Best practice: Update compartment map continuously as water spread evolves.

## Pollution (Code Papa)
Use for environmental incident response:
- Pollution type and source
- Onboard location and sea position
- Estimated volume and severity
- Wind/current and drift estimates
- Response/checklist follow-up

Best practice: Keep environmental data updated for external reporting accuracy.

## Medical (Code Mike)
Use for casualty and medical response tracking:
- Patient status and location
- Medical team actions
- Escalation and support requests

Best practice: Keep patient movement and condition updates short, structured, and frequent.

## Muster
Use for accountability during general alarm:
- Expected vs accounted passengers/crew
- Missing counts in real time
- Flowchart-driven sequence
- Abandon signal timer integration

Best practice: Prioritize reliable count quality over speed; avoid duplicate counting.

## Abandon Ship
Use when evacuation to craft is required:
- Phase progression
- On board vs embarked vs missing
- Craft readiness and launch progression
- Accounting panel and announcement/timer support

Best practice: Keep craft assignment and embarked totals synchronized after each wave.

## OSC
On-Scene Commander operational page:
- OSC identity and tactical position
- Incident type context
- Bridge communication presets
- Dedicated Fire and Protection team panels

Best practice: Use OSC page as tactical command node, not as passive dashboard.

## Plans
Deck-by-deck visual navigation:
- Select deck and plan type (GA / Fire control)
- Zoom, pan, rotate for tactical orientation

Best practice: Use standardized location naming when reporting from plans.

## Lists
Crew/passenger reference and accountability support.

## Messages
Internal communication channel:
- Quick predefined messages
- Broadcast chat
- Message history

Best practice: Use short, action-oriented messages with location first.

## Log and Report
- Log captures operational timeline
- Report page compiles structured incident output

Best practice: Ensure major decisions and status transitions are logged promptly.

## Settings / Network
- Enable network sync
- Configure server IP, port, token
- Reconnect/disconnect controls
- Profile and station management

Best practice: Verify network connectivity before drills or incidents.

## 5. Recommended workflow during a real event
1. Activate incident from Home.
2. Open the related emergency page immediately.
3. Enter initial location, severity, and immediate hazards.
4. Lock down barriers (doors/ventilation) as required.
5. Assign and track teams.
6. Maintain muster/accountability in parallel.
7. Keep OSC, Messages, and Log updated continuously.
8. Save report snapshots at key milestones.

---

## 6. Good operational habits
- One operator updates one module at a time to reduce conflicts.
- Use consistent wording for locations and zones.
- Update statuses in real time, not in bulk later.
- Treat the Log as a legal/operational timeline.
- Perform short refresh cycles (for example every 2 to 5 minutes during escalation).

---

## 7. Quick training drill script (15 minutes)
1. Activate Fire scenario from Home.
2. Enter fire location and evacuation zone.
3. Mark fire/watertight doors and ventilation status.
4. Assign fire teams and update checklist.
5. Add 1 casualty and pass to Medical.
6. Start Muster and enter partial accounting.
7. Escalate to Abandon and assign craft progression.
8. Review Log and generate final report snapshot.
